## Why Seminole Heights Residents Need a Water Softener

The Seminole Heights Residential District is on the National Register of Historic Places, and the National Park Service lists its architectural styles as Bungalow/Craftsman and Gothic Revival with 1912 as its significant year. The district is noted for the period from 1912 to 1928, so many homes here are a century old. A bungalow of that age can have original, repaired or fully replaced plumbing, and a free test and a look at the pipes decide what we recommend.

The Tampa Water Department's 2025 water quality report put average total hardness at 12.3 grains per gallon, about 211 mg/L, which the U.S. Geological Survey rates as very hard, and its 2024 report put the average at 10.8 grains per gallon. At 12.3 gpg, a household of four using 320 gallons a day removes about 3,900 grains daily, so a 32,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 8 days.

## About Seminole Heights Water Quality

The Tampa Water Department draws primarily from the Hillsborough River, and it adds chlorine and then ammonia to create chloramine. Its 2025 report gave a daily average of 3.6 ppm for chlorine and chloramines and an average pH of 7.88. The City of Tampa says hardness runs between about 140 and 300 ppm, or 8 to 17 grains per gallon, lower in the wet season and higher in the dry season. The report is citywide and gives no Seminole Heights figure, so a test at your tap gives the number for your home.
